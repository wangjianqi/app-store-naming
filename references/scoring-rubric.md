# Naming scoring rubric

Scores are comparison aids, not mathematical truth.

Hard gates override score:

- clear existing-name collision
- high third-party trademark/brand risk
- Apple metadata violation
- misleading product claim

## 1. ASO relevance & search intent — 25

Evaluate whether the name naturally connects to the user’s likely search intent without keyword stuffing.

- 21–25: strongly aligned with a high-value core intent and still reads naturally
- 16–20: good relevance but weaker intent coverage or slightly generic
- 10–15: some relevance, mostly dependent on subtitle
- 0–9: weak/unclear search relevance

Do not invent keyword volume.

## 2. Brand distinctiveness — 20

- 17–20: ownable, memorable, visually distinctive, easy to recognize
- 13–16: solid but somewhat familiar/generic
- 8–12: difficult to distinguish from category peers
- 0–7: generic, derivative, or confusingly similar

## 3. Product clarity — 15

- 13–15: user can infer core value quickly
- 10–12: clear with subtitle support
- 6–9: ambiguous but recoverable
- 0–5: misleading or opaque

## 4. Memorability / pronunciation / typing — 10

Consider:

- pronunciation
- spelling recall
- typing friction
- verbal sharing
- ambiguity between similar letters/sounds

## 5. Future extensibility — 10

Does the name survive realistic expansion over the next 1–3 years?

Avoid names tied unnecessarily to:

- one textbook
- one provider
- one language
- one file format
- one temporary feature

when the roadmap is broader.

## 6. Competitive uniqueness — 10

Check current category names and adjacent brands. Distinctiveness is about practical market confusion, not only exact matching.

## 7. Localization robustness — 5

Check:

- meaning in target languages
- pronunciation
- transliteration ambiguity
- accidental negative/awkward meanings
- whether literal translation should be avoided

## 8. App Store presentation fit — 5

Check how the name works in:

- search results
- product page header
- screenshots/marketing copy
- spoken recommendations
- compact UI contexts

## Interpreting totals

- 90–100: unusually strong finalist, still subject to availability/legal gates
- 80–89: strong candidate
- 70–79: viable but has a meaningful trade-off
- 60–69: needs revision or stronger subtitle/positioning
- <60: generally reject

Do not present the score as objective market truth.
